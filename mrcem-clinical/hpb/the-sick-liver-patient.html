The sick liver patient

etiologies

  • existing liver disease? Acute vs chronic liver failure
  • drugs
  • alcohol
  • viral infections
  • sepsizzzz
  • etc:
    • HELLP
    • vascular
    • metabolic, Wilson's disease
    • autoimmune
    • ...sometimes idiopathic
    • toxins: amanita

king's college criteria

  • pH < 7.3 or INR > 6.5 (PT > 100s)
  • Cr > 300micromol/L
  • grade III or IV encephalopathy

management strategies

airway

  • if their consciousness level is so low, then RSI may be the only way forward

breathing

  • potential hazards: pulmonary aspiration; Pleural effusion

circulation

  • hyperdynamic circulation
  • inotropes if required - norad
  • albumin?

disability

  • avoid sedatives if possible
    • go for drugs that do not require hepatic metabolism - remi vs fent
    • short half lives are your friend - propofol
  • see hepatic encephalopathy in acute liver failure
    • opposite pathology compared to alcohol withdrawal
  • risk of cerebral oedema (compared to the Decompensated cirrhosis patients)

exposure

fluids

  • electrolyte management → prevent ascites
  • co-existing renal failure - depending on etiology, or as part of hepatorenal syndrome

gastro

  • Risk hypoglycaemia
  • thiamine needs to be given with glucose
  • Optimise Nutrition in ICU - 35-40kcal/kg/day?

haem

  • potential hazards: Upper GI bleed

infection

  • Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
